# Street quality study

## Purpose and status

This follow-up study improves the visual prototype as a playable street scene.
It concentrates on the view from the bicycle: a stronger early-evening mood,
more readable street depth, richer project-authored assets, and a compact game
HUD that leaves most of the world visible.

The implementation and bounded production review are complete in the current
checkout. This report records that follow-up without replacing the earlier
visual, delivery, and minimap milestone records.

## Reference and design intent

The supplied quality reference shows a bicycle delivery game at dusk with a
quiet residential street, strong warm and cool separation, pools of lamplight,
dense foreground detail, and very little interface chrome. It is a quality and
composition reference. No image, model, texture, character, layout, or branded
element from that reference is copied into Colombo Delivery.

The implemented direction uses the same broad principles where they suit this
prototype:

- the bicycle, road, and next stretch of street remain the main composition;
- warm architecture and lights sit against a cooler sky and shaded ground;
- repeated façades gain enough silhouette and surface variation to read at the
  following-camera distance;
- route objective, speed, and minimap remain legible without covering the
  street; and
- source and performance diagnostics remain available in Inspect mode rather
  than competing with normal play.

This pass does not reach reference parity. It has no hand-painted texture set,
rain, wet-road response, weather particles, traffic simulation, finished
character rig, authored character animation set, or dense hand-placed
residential environment. The rider remains a procedural temporary model. The
scene remains a bounded practice vignette around saved road data rather than a
finished Colombo level.

## Original asset and surface pipeline

All scenery remains original project artwork generated by
`art/generate_colombo_scenery.py`. The script writes the editable Blender file,
the production GLB, and its manifest. It uses a shared painted material palette
and modeled details such as plaster bands, roof ribs, shutter slats, awnings,
trim, and faceted foliage. It does not download or embed external texture
inputs.

The runtime adds small procedural canvas textures for asphalt, ground, and
paving. These low-resolution repeatable surfaces are created locally in the
browser, use restrained noise or paving marks, and are disposed with the scene.
They break up large flat planes without changing road geometry, collision, or
the metre-based world scale. They are a lightweight surface treatment, not a
photographic material library.

The asset kit still uses named cloneable GLB roots and shared resources. The
current quality pass extends modeled detail and improves silhouettes within
that established export contract. The final Blender export contains 65 nodes,
55 meshes, 20 materials, 28,028 vertices, and 47,822 triangles. The production
GLB is approximately 3.4 MB and the editable Blender file is approximately
580 KB.

Direct glTF base colours preserve the accepted palette. The GLB does not
contain imported or generated image textures; its surface character comes from
materials and modeled geometry. Asphalt, paving, and ground variation are the
separate browser-generated canvas textures described above.

## Street and lighting treatment

The scene now combines a cool upper sky, warm horizon light, stronger
directional shadows, textured roads and ground, paved footway treatment, and a
simple distant city edge. The intent is to keep the route readable while
giving the small street slice foreground, middle-distance, and horizon layers.

Decorative scenery does not add gameplay collision. Placement remains derived
from saved road tangents with explicit setbacks and conservative checks against
ground roads. It does not claim that a generated shop, tree, light, wall,
footway, tuk-tuk, or skyline object exists at that real position. The Lotus
Tower remains the separately anchored landmark at the mapped origin.

## Compact game HUD

Ride mode now uses small dark translucent overlays influenced by the reference's
visual economy. The top-left cluster contains a compact product label, speed,
and the active practice run. The delivery card keeps the current objective,
time, reward context, and native action button. Its visible `Practice run`
label retains the unverified context on narrow screens where the longer caveat
is hidden.

The north-up practice minimap remains available at the lower left. Its expanded
state keeps a visible legend for the bicycle, pickup, and drop-off markers;
its collapsed mobile state shrinks to the accessible Map toggle. Touch steering,
pedal, brake, reset, keyboard controls, delivery actions, and phase changes are
unchanged. The reset control becomes icon-only on mobile while retaining its
accessible name.

Road counts, FPS, and scenery status remain visible in Inspect mode. A scenery
load failure is the exception: it remains pinned in Ride mode after transient
feedback expires so visual polish cannot hide a runtime problem.

The final street composition contains 72 deterministic decorative placements:
29 mature trees, 26 frontage pieces, one instanced shrub batch, and one
instanced five-pool lamplight batch. The instance batches reduce repeated draw
work while keeping shrubs and light pools decorative.

## Validation

Strict TypeScript checking, all 23 tests, the production build, and the
documentation diff check pass.

Production browser review covered:

- the complete first keyboard delivery over 67 m, producing LKR 240 and one
  completion, followed by the next available practice job;
- switching to Inspect and back without changing the delivery or bicycle state;
- the 390 × 844 layout with no horizontal overflow, an expanded and collapsed
  minimap, its visible marker legend, pointer-held pedal above 4 km/h, and Reset
  returning the bicycle to 0 km/h;
- a deliberately aborted GLB request on mobile, where the persistent scenery
  failure remained visible after the temporary message expired while Accept
  and Collect remained usable; and
- empty scoped console-warning, console-error, and page-error captures during
  the normal desktop and mobile runs.

A short local desktop sample captured 120 animation frames after the delivery
while stationary. Its median interval was 16.7 ms and its 95th percentile was
17.1 ms; the comparison baseline was 16.7 ms median and 17.6 ms at the 95th
percentile. This is a narrow local smoke measurement, not a sustained-play,
representative-hardware, or general 60 FPS guarantee.

The reviewed images are the
[desktop street-quality frame](milestones/2026-09-13-street-quality.jpg),
[mobile street-quality frame](milestones/2026-09-13-street-quality-mobile.jpg),
and [neutral browser asset preview](milestones/2026-09-13-street-quality-assets.jpg).
The desktop and mobile frames show the next job available after the prior QA
delivery, with saved totals of LKR 240 and one completion. The asset image is a
browser capture of `art-preview.html` under neutral asset lighting rather than
the production evening scene. That browser view scales the Lotus Tower to
`0.035`, approximately 1:28.6, so all ten roots fit. The separately generated
`public/models/colombo_scenery_preview.png` uses the reproducible Blender
contact-sheet renderer and previews the tower at `0.025`, or 1:40.

The production build emits a 78.51 KB main entry (26.92 KB gzip), a 630.17 KB
shared JavaScript chunk (159.38 KB gzip), a 3.06 KB preview entry (1.70 KB
gzip), 22.99 KB CSS (5.38 KB gzip), and the 2,879.94 KB saved GeoJSON artifact.
Vite retains its expected advisory for the shared output above 500 KB.

Longer rides, physical-device multi-touch, representative-hardware performance,
other browsers, rain and weather, traffic, final character art, animation, and
broader environment production remain outside this bounded pass.
